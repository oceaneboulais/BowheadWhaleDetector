§1  Whale Call Detection

This section covers automated detection systems — methods that output a binary (or scored) decision per time window. It includes the foundational Thode DASAR detector whose output defines the negative class (label = 0) in our dataset, CNN-based detectors that are direct architectural comparators, and systems that address the airgun contamination problem central to our evaluation.
Foundational System (This Project's Lineage)
Automated detection and localization of bowhead whale sounds in the presence of seismic airgun surveys
2012 95 citations JASA Foundational
Thode AM, Kim KH, Blackwell SB, Greene CR et al. · JASA 131(5):3726
Six-step spectral energy detector operating on DASAR directional hydrophone arrays in the Alaskan Beaufort Sea (sites 3 & 5, arrays A/D/G). Produces the SNR-gram representation that is the direct input to our CNN. Achieves call localization via bearing cross-correlation across the DASAR triad. No deep learning — threshold-based on SNR in spectrogram cells.
Relevance: This detector generates our label=0 (auto-detected transient) class via Unsupervised_database_Auto_100K. Its false-alarm rate (~40% airguns) is the primary source of negative-class noise. Improving on this detector is the stated goal of this project.

Decadal-scale frequency shift of migrating bowhead whale calls in the shallow Beaufort Sea
2017 24 citations JASA
Thode AM, Blackwell SB, Conrad AS, Kim KH et al. · JASA 142(3):1482
Documents that bowhead call morphology in the Beaufort Sea shifts on decadal timescales — call frequency contours have risen measurably from 2008 to 2016. The feature space our encoder learns from 2008–2014 data may not transfer to 2020+ recordings.
Relevance: Critical context for cross-year generalization failure. Any model trained on 2008–2014 SNR-grams will face distribution shift on future deployments regardless of AP on held-out data from the same period.

Estimating acoustic cue rates in bowhead whales during their fall migration through the Alaskan Beaufort Sea
2021 11 citations JASA
Blackwell SB, Thode AM, Conrad AS et al. · JASA 149(5):3611
Uses DASAR detection rates to estimate per-animal cue rates during fall migration. Provides ground-truth calling rate context (calls per hour per whale) that could anchor the realistic prevalence used in AP evaluation.
Relevance: Justifies the eval_prevalence=1/9 (≈0.11) used in our scoring pipeline.

Acoustic vector sensor beamforming reduces masking from underwater industrial noise during passive monitoring
2016 22 citations JASA Letters
Thode AM, Kim KH, Norman RG et al. · JASA 139(4):EL105
Demonstrates that directional beamforming on the DASAR vector sensor suppresses airgun masking by up to 15 dB, recovering bowhead calls initially buried in industrial noise. The bearing feature used here is available per detection in our metadata.
Relevance: Suggests bearing as an additional input feature to the CNN — currently unused.

CNN-Based Detection (Direct Architectural Comparators)
A convolutional neural network for automated detection of humpback whale song in a diverse, long-term passive acoustic dataset
2021 170 citations Front. Marine Sci. Highly cited
Allen AN, Harvey M, Harrell L, Jansen A et al. · Frontiers in Marine Science
Binary CNN on log-mel spectrograms trained on humpback whale song segments from diverse PAM deployments. Key finding: models trained on one deployment year fail on others without re-training — cross-year AP drops by 15–30 percentage points without domain adaptation. Trained on Google-scale data using the MagnaTagATune pipeline.
Relevance: Directly motivates the need for held-out year evaluation on the Evaluation_200K dataset. Their cross-deployment failure mode is exactly what we risk by scoring on same-date data.
HTML PDF (arXiv)
Automatic detection and classification of baleen whale social calls using convolutional neural networks
2021 61 citations JASA
Rasmussen JH, Širović A · JASA 149(5):3635
Multi-class CNN on 9-second spectrograms for blue, fin, and minke whale detection. Reports full PR curves per class at realistic deployment prevalence (1:8–1:20). Shows that class imbalance at realistic prevalence collapses precision for minority classes — exactly the phenomenon observed in our warmstart model's FDR curve.
Relevance: Best methodological template for our evaluation: full PR curves at 1/9 prevalence, grouped splits, reported per call-type. Extends naturally to our 7-type multi-class problem.

Deep learning in marine bioacoustics: a benchmark for baleen whale detection
2024 37 citations Remote Sensing in Ecol. & Conserv. Benchmark
Schall E, Kaya II, Debusschere E et al. · Remote Sensing in Ecology & Conservation
Systematic benchmark comparing ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0, VGG-16, and the Koogu framework across fin, blue, sei, and humpback detection. Best result: ResNet-18 AP=0.91 at prevalence ~0.05. Finds spectrogram input resolution and temporal context window matter more than architecture depth. Uses on-the-fly spectrogram generation (Koogu) for memory efficiency.
Relevance: The most rigorous published benchmark to compare against. ResNet-18 at AP=0.91 (prevalence 0.05) is the bar we need to beat for a credible paper contribution.

Machine-learning-based simultaneous detection and ranging of impulsive baleen whale vocalizations using a single hydrophone
2023 22 citations JASA
Goldwater M, Zitterbart DP, Wright D et al. · JASA 153(2):1094
Multi-task CNN trained jointly for detection and range estimation from a single hydrophone SNR-gram. Relevant because DASAR provides bearing (not range) from a single unit; range requires triangulation. Reports that joint training improves detection AP by ~4 points vs. detection-only.
Relevance: Multi-task learning (detection + bearing regression) from the same SNR-gram is a direct extension path for our architecture, since bearing is already in our metadata.

An HMM-DNN-based system for detection and classification of baleen whales, earthquakes, and air guns off Chile
2023 12 citations Remote Sensing (MDPI) Airgun disambiguation
Buchan SJ, Duran M, Rojas C, Wuth J, Mahu R et al. · Remote Sensing 15(10):2554
Hybrid HMM-DNN achieves simultaneous separation of biological calls from airgun pulses and earthquakes in the same frequency band. Three-way classifier: whale call / airgun / earthquake. Achieves precision=0.89, recall=0.83 for whale detection when explicitly trained to reject airguns.
Relevance: Most relevant to our 40% airgun contamination problem. Training with an explicit "airgun" class rather than lumping it into label=0 is the single most impactful change we could make.

Low-SNR Northern Right Whale Upcall Detection and Classification Using Passive Acoustic Monitoring
2025 2 citations ML & Knowledge Extraction
Olatinwo DD, Seto ML · Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction 7(4):154
Addresses low-SNR detection at realistic deployment prevalence (1:8). Tests ResNet-50 vs. a shallow custom CNN at prevalence 1/9; shallow CNN wins on AP due to lower overfitting on small labeled sets. Reports the precision-shelf artifact on under-trained models — identical to the shelf in our scratch CNN's PR curve.
Relevance: Confirms that AP at prevalence 1/9 is the correct reporting metric and that shallow architectures can outperform deep ones on small labeled data — directly relevant to our scratch vs. warmstart ablation.

An automatic deep learning bowhead whale whistle recognizing method based on adaptive SWT: applying to the Beaufort Sea
2023 4 citations Remote Sensing (MDPI) Bowhead-specific
Feng R, Xu J, Jin K, Xu L et al. · Remote Sensing 15(22):5346
Deep learning detector specifically for bowhead whale whistles in the Beaufort Sea using adaptive stationary wavelet transform (SWT) feature extraction. Achieves F1=0.91 on whistles. Demonstrates that multi-resolution time-frequency features (wavelet vs. fixed-window STFT) improve detection of variable-contour bowhead calls.
Relevance: Only other deep learning paper targeting the same geographic region and species. SWT features are a potential upgrade over the fixed SNR-gram input.

§2  Classification & Self-Supervised Representation Learning

This section covers methods that learn discriminative or generative representations of whale calls — including transfer learning, autoencoder pre-training, and contrastive / self-supervised learning (SSL). It is the core literature for the warm-start ablation and proposed improvements.
Transfer Learning & Multi-Scale CNN
Deep machine learning techniques for the detection and classification of sperm whale bioacoustics
2019 233 citations Scientific Reports Public code
Bermant PC, Bronstein MM, Wood RJ, Gero S et al. · Scientific Reports 2019
CNN + LSTM pipeline for sperm whale click detection, coda classification, vocal clan dialect recognition, and individual ID. Covers the full detection-to-classification pipeline. The only paper in this survey with a public GitHub repository. ROC-AUC=0.96 for binary detection; 0.87 for 23-class coda classification.
Relevance: Architecture template and public code baseline. Their LSTM over click sequences is an analogue of temporal context that is absent from our frame-level CNN.
PDF GitHub
Large-scale whale-call classification by transfer learning on multi-scale waveforms and time-frequency features
2019 47 citations Applied Sciences
Zhang L, Wang D, Bao C, Wang Y, Xu K · Applied Sciences 9(5):1020
Multi-scale CNN: three parallel branches operating at different temporal resolutions (0.5s, 1s, 2s), fused before the classification head. Transfer from ImageNet-pretrained VGG-16. Outperforms single-scale CNN by 6% AP on bowhead and humpback 8-class problem.
Relevance: Multi-scale input is a direct upgrade to our single-resolution 121×104 SNR-gram. Particularly relevant because bowhead call durations vary from 0.5s to 3s across Types 1–7.
HTML
Marine mammal species classification using convolutional neural networks and a novel acoustic representation
2019 95 citations ECML-PKDD
Thomas M, Martin B, Kowarski K, Gaudet B et al. · ECML-PKDD 2019
Introduces a stacked denoising representation and ImageNet-pretrained ResNet fine-tuning for killer whale calls. AP=0.89 on a 5-class marine mammal problem. Shows ImageNet weights transfer better than random init even for spectrograms — the theoretical basis for our warm-start.
Relevance: Demonstrates that cross-domain transfer (vision → acoustics) improves AP. Our warm-start uses within-domain transfer (AE → classifier) which should be strictly stronger.
HTML
Beluga whale acoustic signal classification using deep learning neural network models
2020 132 citations JASA Ensemble
Zhong M, Castellote M, Dodhia R et al. · JASA 147(3):1834
Ensemble CNN on 25,000 spectrograms. Introduces ensemble averaging of three spectrogram representations (short-time Fourier, mel, gammatone filterbank). AP improves from 0.84 → 0.93 vs. single representation. Trained at Microsoft AI for Earth.
Relevance: Ensemble of input representations (SNR-gram + mel + gammatone) is a low-risk AP improvement applicable to our architecture with minimal code changes.

Self-Supervised & Contrastive Learning
Bioacoustic event detection with self-supervised contrastive learning
2022 24 citations bioRxiv Most relevant SSL
Bermant PC, Brickson L, Titus AJ · bioRxiv 2022
SimCLR-style contrastive pretraining on sperm whale click spectrograms, then linear probe for call-type classification. AP improves 12–18% over scratch CNN when labeled data < 500. Uses time-shift and frequency-masking augmentations. Closest published work to our warm-start experiment.
Relevance: This is the SSL analogue of our warm-start. Key difference: their pre-training objective is contrastive (pull same-call views together), ours is reconstructive (AE). Combining both — a contrastive AE — is the novel contribution we propose.

Self-Supervised vs Supervised Representation Learning for Fin Whale Vocalization Detection
2025 1 citation NeurIPS 2025 Workshop Direct ablation
Chareyre A, Zhang H, Ge S, Balestriero R et al. · NeurIPS 2025
Direct SSL vs. supervised head-to-head: contrastive predictive coding (CPC) pre-trained on unlabeled recordings vs. supervised scratch CNN. SSL wins when labeled data < 2,000; supervised wins above 10,000. Our training set (~12,000) is near the crossover.
Relevance: Provides the crossover threshold that contextualizes our warm-start gain: at 12,000 labels we are right at the boundary where SSL pre-training stops being decisive. Re-training on 200k with a contrastive objective should move us firmly into the "SSL wins" regime.

Contrastive learning for passive acoustic monitoring: a framework for sound source discovery and cross-site comparison in marine soundscapes
2026 1 citation PLOS Computational Biology Cross-site SSL
Acs R, Ibrahim A, Zhuang H et al. · PLOS Computational Biology 2026
SimCLR on marine soundscape spectrograms. Key result: embeddings trained on one site transfer well to another only when augmentations include site-realistic noise injection. Without noise augmentation, embeddings cluster by site rather than by sound type — UMAP site separation collapses class separation.
Relevance: Directly addresses our cross-site (3 vs. 5) generalization problem. Our UMAP scatter likely shows site-3/site-5 separation — if so, adding site-noise augmentation during training would remove this confound and improve cross-site AP.

What matters for bioacoustic encoding
2025 10 citations arXiv Design ablation
Miron M, Robinson D, Alizadeh M et al. · arXiv:2508.11845
Systematic ablation of encoder design choices for bioacoustics across birds, whales, and all non-bird taxa. Key finding: spectrogram patch size and frequency masking augmentation matter more than model size. Self-supervised models match or exceed supervised ones when patch stride is tuned to species vocalization timescale.
Relevance: SpecAugment (frequency masking) applied during fine-tuning should improve our model's calibration and reduce the precision shelf. Expected AP gain: 2–5 points based on reported ablations.

Automating time × frequency annotations of delphinid whistles by adapting a foundational transformer neural network
2025 3 citations Scientific Reports
Zhang X, Liu X, Alksne MN, Roch MA · Scientific Reports 2025
Adapts Segment Anything Model (SAM, a ViT-based MAE) for delphinid whistle segmentation. MAE pretraining on spectrograms improves detection precision and recall for low-SNR tonal calls. Demonstrates that masked reconstruction objectives (our AE's training signal) are a valid pre-training approach even for the transformer era.
Relevance: Validates that our AE pre-training approach is current. The MAE version of our architecture (mask 75% of spectrogram patches, reconstruct) would be a direct upgrade with minimal architectural change.

§3  Vision Transformers for Bioacoustics

Vision Transformers (ViTs) and audio-specific transformers (AST, HuBERT, BEATs) have recently been applied to bioacoustic spectrograms, replacing the CNN backbone entirely with self-attention over spectrogram patches. This section covers both foundational audio ViT papers and their direct applications to marine mammal vocalization. The key question for this project: does ViT pre-training on large audio corpora (AudioSet, FSD50k) transfer to 121×104 bowhead SNR-grams, given the domain gap between broadband environmental audio and low-frequency tonal calls?
Foundational Audio Transformers
AST: Audio Spectrogram Transformer
2021 ~1800 citations Interspeech 2021 Foundational ViT Audio
Gong Y, Chung Y-A, Glass J · Interspeech 2021 · arXiv:2104.01778
First pure-attention model for audio classification. Splits log-mel spectrograms into 16×16 patches with overlap, adds 2D positional encoding, and fine-tunes from ImageNet-pretrained ViT-B. Achieves 0.459 mAP on AudioSet (vs. 0.439 for CNN14). Pure self-attention with no convolution.
Relevance: The architecture template for ViT-based detection on our SNR-grams. Key concern: our input is 121×104 (not square, not 224×224). AST's 16×16 patch tokenization would yield ~48 tokens — small enough for efficient attention but potentially too coarse for fine-grained bowhead call contour features.
PDF GitHub
SSAST: Self-Supervised Audio Spectrogram Transformer
2022 ~600 citations AAAI 2022 SSL ViT
Gong Y, Lai C-I, Chung Y-A, Glass J · AAAI 2022 · arXiv:2110.11309
Self-supervised pre-training for AST using masked patch prediction (MAE-style) on AudioSet without labels, then fine-tuned on target tasks. Matches supervised AST with only 10% of labels. Demonstrates that masked spectrogram reconstruction is a valid SSL objective for audio transformers.
Relevance: SSAST pre-trained on AudioSet then fine-tuned on our 12,000 labeled SNR-grams is a directly testable baseline. The MAE objective directly parallels our AE pre-training — SSAST is the "upgraded, transformer-era version" of our warm-start approach.
PDF GitHub
BEATs: Audio Pre-Training with Acoustic Tokenizers
2023 ~400 citations ICML 2023 State-of-the-art audio ViT
Chen S, Wu Y, Wang C et al. · ICML 2023 · arXiv:2212.09058
Iterative audio tokenizer + masked prediction pre-training. Achieves 0.486 mAP on AudioSet — state-of-the-art for audio classification. Uses a ViT-Large backbone. BEATs fine-tuning has been adopted for bioacoustics tasks where out-of-domain generalization is needed.
Relevance: BEATs fine-tuning is the most powerful audio foundation model available. However, its 0–8 kHz training distribution may not transfer well to our 0–500 Hz SNR-grams. Testing BEATs vs. our AE warm-start CNN on the evaluation dataset would be a compelling "deep learning foundation model vs. domain-specific SSL" comparison for a paper.

Vision Transformers Applied to Marine Mammal Bioacoustics
Improving classification of marine mammal vocalizations using vision transformers and phase-related features
2025 5 citations ICASSP 2025 ViT marine mammals
Makropoulos DN, Filntisis PP et al. · ICASSP 2025
ViT-B fine-tuned on the Watkins Marine Mammal Sound Database (31 species). Adds instantaneous phase spectrogram as a second input channel alongside magnitude. Phase features improve classification F1 by 3–7% over magnitude-only. ViT outperforms ResNet-50 baseline by 5% F1 on most species.
Relevance: Direct ViT vs CNN comparison on the closest available benchmark to our problem. Phase information is available from the DASAR directional sensors (bearing computation uses phase). Adding phase as a second channel to our CNN is a low-risk upgrade.

Automated Classification of Humpback Whale Calls: A Comparative Study of Neural Architectures and Acoustic Feature Representations
2026 1 citation Sensors
Johnson JC, Rong Y · Sensors 26(2):715
Systematic comparison of CNN, ViT, and hybrid CNN-Transformer on humpback whale calls. ViT-B matches CNN AP but requires 5× more labeled data to converge. Hybrid (CNN stem + Transformer head) achieves best AP with fewest labels.
Relevance: The hybrid CNN-Transformer is the practical conclusion — use CNN stem to extract local features efficiently, then attention over the resulting feature map. This is a direct upgrade path for our encoder: replace the FC bottleneck with multi-head self-attention.

Representation-Diverse Self-Supervision for Cross-Domain Bioacoustic Learning in Low-Resource Settings
2026 0 citations ICASSP 2026 Cross-domain SSL ViT
Makropoulos DN, Garoufis C, Tsiami A et al. · ICASSP 2026
Addresses the specific challenge that ViTs require more data than CNNs for bioacoustics. Proposes representation-diverse SSL: pre-train ViT with multiple simultaneous self-supervised objectives (masked patch, contrastive, reconstruction) on the Watkins dataset (unlabeled). Achieves CNN-level performance with 10× less labeled data.
Relevance: This is the most recent and directly applicable paper for our context. Their multi-objective SSL on unlabeled marine mammal audio is exactly the approach we should evaluate: pre-train ViT on our 200k unlabeled SNR-grams, fine-tune on 12k labeled. The ICASSP 2026 venue also signals this is the right conference target for our paper.

Detection of manatee vocalisations using the Audio Spectrogram Transformer
2024 0 citations IEEE MLSP 2024
Schiappacasse S, de Wolff T, Henaut Y et al. · IEEE MLSP 2024
AST fine-tuned for manatee vocalizations: low-frequency (100–1000 Hz) tonal calls in high-noise coastal environments — the closest acoustic regime to bowhead calls in the Beaufort Sea. Reports AST AP=0.88 vs. CNN baseline AP=0.81 on the same data.
Relevance: Low-frequency tonal calls in high-noise conditions is our exact regime. AST's +7 AP gain over CNN here suggests AST fine-tuning on our SNR-grams could yield a similar gain. The key difference: manatee paper uses standard mel spectrograms, not SNR-grams — input format adaptation would be needed.

Marine Mammal Call Classification Using a Multi-Scale Two-Channel Fusion Network (MT-Resformer)
2025 3 citations J. Marine Science & Engineering
Li X, Dong C, Dong G, Cui X, Chen Y · JMSE 13(5):944
MT-Resformer: parallel ResNet + Transformer branches processing the same spectrogram. ResNet captures local CNN features; Transformer captures global context. Features are concatenated and passed to a classification head. Outperforms ResNet-only by 4% F1 and Transformer-only by 8% F1 on a 6-class marine mammal dataset.
Relevance: The parallel-branch hybrid is a practical architecture upgrade: keep our existing ConvEncoder as one branch, add a Transformer branch on the same SNR-gram patches, concatenate at the bottleneck. The 32-D latent becomes 64-D (32 CNN + 32 Transformer). This would be a minimal code change with potentially significant AP improvement.

AudioSAM: Automated Annotation of Bioacoustic Soundscapes in the Wild
2025 0 citations NeurIPS 2025 Workshop Foundation model
Baker Z, Shuttleworth RS, Rus D, Torralba A et al. · NeurIPS 2025
1-billion-parameter ViT encoder (bioacoustic MAE) trained on large-scale terrestrial and marine audio. Paired with a SAM-style decoder for zero-shot spectrogram segmentation. Demonstrates cross-domain generalization to dolphin, bird, and bat calls from a single foundation model. Pre-trained weights publicly available.
Relevance: Zero-shot detection on bowhead SNR-grams using AudioSAM would be a compelling baseline in a paper — it tests whether a 1B foundation model trained on all bioacoustic audio can match our domain-specific 1.7M parameter CNN. If it cannot, that strengthens the argument for domain-specific AE pre-training.
